Foodie Book Privacy Policy

Last updated November 5, 2026

This policy explains what Foodie Book collects, why, and who it's shared with. Foodie Book has no ads, does not track you across other apps or websites, and never sells your data.

What we store

Your account. An anonymous account ID is created when you first open the app. If you create an account, we also store your email address and your first and last name. With Sign in with Apple, the email can be a private relay address that hides your real one, and your name is only stored if you choose to share it.

What you add. Recipes (text, photos, links), pantry items, grocery lists and shopping history, meal plans, macro targets, prices you record, products you scan, albums, and household membership. This is stored so the app works across launches and devices.

App version and activity. When you open the app, we save which version of Foodie Book and iOS you're using and when you last opened it, with your account ID. This helps us know who has the latest update and fix problems.

Receipts. Receipt photos are read on your iPhone. Only the item names and prices you confirm are saved — the photo isn't uploaded. The same goes for receipt screenshots and product photos you upload to your pantry: they're read on your iPhone (text and barcodes) and not uploaded.

Your store and location. If you set a home store, we save the store you pick (its name, street address and map position), your city and, if you enter it, your ZIP code. While you scan products, the app can check your iPhone's location — only if you allow it — to suggest the grocery store you're at. Your location is used on your iPhone to look up nearby stores with Apple Maps and is never saved or sent to us; only the store you choose is saved with the prices you record.

Product photos. When a recipe video shows a product's package and Open Food Facts has no photo of it, one still frame of the package is saved with your recipe as that product's photo. The video itself is never saved.

Services that process your data

  • Supabase hosts our database and storage.
  • OpenAI — only if you allow it. When you import a recipe, we send the recipe text you imported (a post's caption, pasted text, or text read from your screenshots) so it can be organized into ingredients, steps and servings. When you add a recipe's ingredients to your grocery list, we send those ingredient names and amounts, the recipe titles and the names and amounts of your pantry items, so they can be combined into a clean shopping list without what you already have. When you ask for swaps for an ingredient, we send that ingredient, its amount, the recipe's title and its other ingredients. When you open a meal prep, we send its recipes' ingredient lines and titles so they can be combined into a tidy prep list with prep steps. When you ask for recipe ideas, we send what you asked for, your pantry item names, the titles of recipes you've saved, and the diet and restrictions you set in your profile, so the ideas fit them. When our food database can't match a recipe ingredient, we send its name and amount to estimate its nutrition. When you add a product by photographing its label, we send those photos so the details can be read; the front photo is kept as the product's picture for other shoppers, and the label photos aren't stored. When a receipt line can't be matched on your phone, we send it with your grocery item names to pair them. When you scan a receipt, we send only its item lines (never the totals or payment details) so the items and amounts can be read. When a recipe lives in a video, we also send that video's audio and a few still frames so the recipe, the exact ingredients and product brands can be read. If a video shows the cooking without explaining it, we send up to a dozen still frames, plus any text read off the screen, so Foodie AI can write down what it sees. When you make an invention (Inventions tab), we send what you typed, or the titles, ingredients and steps of the 2–3 recipes you chose to combine, plus the options you picked (servings, time, protein, budget, dietary needs), your home store's name if you turn that on, and what you typed or asked to change, to write the new recipe. When you make an album public, we send each recipe's title, servings, ingredients and steps so Foodie AI can check it's complete and rewrite the steps in plain words for the public copy; the name, city, "about you" and links on your public profile are checked by OpenAI's moderation before they show. Your name, email and other data are not sent. OpenAI does not use API data to train its models. Our temporary copy of the video is deleted right after processing.
  • Spoonacular receives ingredient names (with no personal identifiers, through our server) for nutrition estimates and recipe ideas.
  • Food search uses Foodie Book's own copy of the USDA FoodData Central database; only the food name you type is sent to our backend. When a product isn't in that copy yet, the words you typed (nothing else) are also looked up at USDA FoodData Central, a free U.S. government food database.
  • Open Food Facts receives barcode numbers when you scan a product, and brand and ingredient names (no personal details) when looking up a product's quality score or package size.
  • Social and recipe sites. When you import a link, our server fetches that public post or page (for example TikTok, Instagram, Pinterest, or a recipe blog) to read the recipe.
  • RevenueCat and Apple handle subscriptions. They receive your anonymous app user ID and purchase history. We never see your payment details.
  • Sentry receives crash and error reports so we can fix problems: what went wrong, the app version, and your device model and iOS version. Reports are not linked to your name, email or account, and contain no recipes, photos or IP address.
  • PostHog receives anonymous product analytics so we can see which features are used: which screens are opened (fixed names such as Recipes or Pantry); when a recipe is saved or an import starts, finishes or fails (only its source type, such as website or screenshot, and a short reason such as timeout); a meal is planned (meal slot and whether it uses a saved recipe); cooking mode opens (entry point and whether a video is available); a grocery item, pantry item, barcode or receipt is added or scanned (only how it was added and whether a barcode was found); a shopping trip is finished (whether a receipt was saved); recipe ideas are requested; onboarding is finished; an account is created or Sign in with Apple is used; or the Pro screen is viewed or a Pro purchase starts, completes or is cancelled (only monthly or annual). Events carry the app version and build, device model, iOS version, and a random analytics ID. They are not linked to your name, email, Supabase account or purchase account, and do not include recipe titles, ingredients, pantry items, product names, barcodes, photos or location. PostHog's location lookup, screen recording, surveys, automatic screen tracking and account identification are off.

Sharing with other people

Recipes and lists are private unless you join a household or share an album, in which case members of that household or album can see them. If you turn on community price sharing or add to the community product library, the price, store (including which location), city or product details are shared with other users without your name. If you make an album public on Discover, anyone signed in to Foodie Book can see a reviewed copy of its recipes (title, servings, ingredients, steps in Foodie Book's words, your own photo if you added one, and credit and a link to the original creator), the name you choose to show, and anything you add to your public profile (city, about you, links), plus who you follow, who follows you, and star ratings. Your private notes, email and other recipes are never shown. You can make an album private again at any time, and block people so they can't see your public profile or follow you.

Your choices

Turn video processing on or off in Profile → Privacy. Delete your account and its data anytime in Profile → Delete Account. Questions or requests: ronaldo.gaitan@icloud.com.

Children

Foodie Book isn't directed to children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it here and in the app before the change takes effect.
